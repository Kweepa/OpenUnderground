# Changelog

## Next release

- Drowning works as in the original: every twenty seconds or so a Swimming roll, harder the more you carry, may sink you a little lower in the water, until you begin to drown. With a good Swimming skill and a light pack you can swim as long as you like, with a poor one and a full pack you go under in about a minute. A splash tells you each time you sink, the water hurts with a gulp instead of the grunt and the shake of a blow, which made it feel like an unseen attack, and you climb out by yourself onto any bank no more than a step high, getting your breath back over half a minute.

- The Standard of Honor is thrown like anything else when there is no floor within reach to plant it, out of water and lava: aimed farther away, letting it go did nothing and it stayed in your hand, and in water or lava it was planted upright in it. And a silver seed used where there is no room around you to let it go stays in the pack, with its message, instead of vanishing.

- Taking something that belongs to others makes it yours: drop it and pick it up again and its owners are no longer angered a second time. The name of an owned item is light orange while it is still out of reach, so you see whose it is before the click that takes it. Goods their owners have handed over to you no longer show as theirs.

- Slain creatures leave their body where it fell for at least ten minutes, until you are ten tiles away and cannot see it, instead of vanishing as soon as you look away. When the body goes, it leaves what the original leaves: a pile of bones for a skeleton, rubble for a golem, wood chips for a reaper, and often a skull or a bone that tells whose it was.

- Piles join as in the original: two loaves or two piles of coins of close condition make one pile with weighted average value, and arrows, bolts and sling stones join whatever their condition; a pile holds up to 350, also on the trade table, where it stopped at 99. Coins keep their own condition, so their value is the same as in the original, and piles you kept apart in the pack stay apart after a load.

- The coins around Goldthirst and in his treasure room are gold coins, not silver ones: the dwarf king loves gold and would not surround himself with silver. An addition of ours; they are worth the same.

- Traders haggle as in the original: each wants its own margin over what it thinks the goods are worth, Shak about half again, less with Charm, and every item counts by its condition. The same offer always gets the same answer, so insisting no longer pays, and a trader weary of bad offers is fresh again the next time you talk to it.

- Goldthirst gives his ancestral axe for the large gold nugget when you hand it to him after the monster in the mines is dead, as in the original, instead of keeping it as treasure. And he no longer thanks you for a gift when you have given him nothing.

- With OPL2 chosen as the soundfont the music sounds as it did on an AdLib or Sound Blaster: the game's own FM arrangements of the tracks, with their own instruments, played from your game files on an emulated FM chip. It used to play the MT-32 arrangements through a General MIDI bank, where the timpani was a tinkle bell and the orchestra hit the sound of the sea.

- Character creation puts first the sex and the hand of the character you played last, right-handed if none, and your own choices when you go back or start over: it always proposed the left hand, and Female after you refused a character. Before a game starts, the pad's control map shows that hand's triggers, not always a left-handed one's.

- The map has a notebook on its first page, before Level 1, opened with N or by holding the pad's map button: write the things you have to do, tick one off and it moves down to the Done list, untick it to bring it back. The list scrolls when it grows, and a save keeps it.

- With the GUS soundfont the music plays the instruments it was written for: the timpani is a timpani instead of a carillon, the horns are horns, the orchestra hit is no longer the sound of the sea, the combat music's jungle tune no longer a pistol shot, and the bongos, maracas, claves and tambourine are drums again instead of an out-of-tune blur.

- Four spells are back in their circle, with its mana and level: Leap is first circle, 3 mana from level 1; Thick Skin third, 9 mana from level 5; Levitate and Water Walk fourth, 12 mana from level 7. Levitate and Thick Skin come sooner and cost less, Water Walk later and more.

- The spell list fades the spells you know but cannot cast yet, for a rune missing from your rune bag or a circle above your level, so you see at a glance which ones you can cast.

- The title menu waits for its fade-in to end before taking a choice: an entry picked at once used to open its screen dimmed, sometimes almost black, until you went back to the menu.

- Page Up and Page Down now scroll back through the last two hundred messages, and what was said in conversations is among them, each line after the name of who said it: brown for the people you talk to, orange for you. A save keeps them, so after a load you can read what you were doing and what you were told.

- Garamon's dreams come in the original's order again: the second at the first full sleep away from the first level, the story ones at the first full sleep after their events, none after a sleep a creature interrupted and none once his story is over. The other six now come at a slower pace that grows after each one, and may come again, where before a late event could leave the unseen ones out for good.

- If the prisoner Murgo dies, Sseetharee answers as in the original when asked about him.

- The death cutscene no longer shows a strip of the frame's ornament at the bottom centre of the screen, which looked like the handle of a weapon: the picture was cut out two rows too low.

- A much stronger character no longer keeps a creature flinching with critical after critical so that it never strikes back, as the head bandit of level 3 did: a creature that has flinched now gets its next swing or shot before a critical can make it flinch again.

- A creature a conversation sends to fight you, like a bandit of level 3 after a rude answer, now turns hostile as it attacks instead of staying upset, and no longer talks to you while it means to fight, as in the original: before, you could chat with such a bandit before he noticed you, part on good terms and still be attacked. Ally now stops such a creature, and the creatures that never talk in the original no longer do. A summoned gazer is no longer the named gazer of level 2.

- Lore identifies an item you look at a little less often, as in the original: its roll was the sequel's, one step easier. And a trader's goods on the trade table show at most as magical, as in the original: looking at them no longer reveals what they are.

- Switching to fullscreen and back with Alt+Enter no longer picks the highlighted entry of the title menu. After a click, the pad's attack trigger attacks and its right stick turns the view at once, and a pad left plugged in no longer rumbles while you play with the mouse and keyboard.

- With the MT-32 sound font, seven instruments the font did not have, or had as little more than breath, are no longer played by others: the gun shot in the combat music is a ringing glockenspiel again, the map's accordion a soft bell, the Wanderer's plucked guitar a held brass, Descent's bass a vibraphone and the shakuhachi a pan flute. The horns, the English horn, the harp and the piccolo now play as loud as on an MT-32.

- The music is cleaner with every sound font: held notes lose a slight roughness they had, and it has room for every note, where 12 voices cut the map's music, which plays up to nearly 60, and left out one note in six of the death music.

- When the game's files cannot be unpacked from the copy found at the first start (a full disk, no access), the window now says why instead of saying nothing, and Auto Search tries that copy again once you have made room.

- Holding Enter while typing runes no longer casts the spell over and over, nor does a held letter fill the shelf; holding the cast key, or the pad's cast button, now tries again after a failed roll or until you are ready to cast, and stops at the first spell that goes off: one press, one spell.

- A creature that has fled from you now runs again when it hears you close by, or meets you on its way home, instead of coming to look or turning on you; a fleeing creature jumps down a ledge of up to two metres again, and backs away from you at the original's speed, about twice as fast as before.

- Gaining a level with the weapon raised now plays its fanfare, a creature that wakes you starts the combat music at once, and the next game no longer opens with the warning music after a death with the weapon up, nor is a blow held as you die swung; with the Alert music switched off, raising the weapon no longer cuts the music. The title music now plays once and the menu then goes on with an ambient track, as in the original. A damaged track of your own now just ends where it breaks, and a track you listened to and then removed is deleted from the Music folder.

- An ear of corn on the lit torch you hold now makes popcorn, and on the pad incense burns on it instead of putting it out; combining two items no longer fails after part of a pile was taken, and a refused incense burn no longer loses a block.

- A pile of torches or candles in the inventory now lights one of them, taking off a lit light now lets its snuffing be heard and an unlit one is silent, a key taken off a pile keeps its "(level N)", and on the pad the inventory's A hint now says it also picks an item up.

- After a sleep the mana no longer fills up by itself in two seconds, nor does a ring of Regeneration heal you fully, as in the original; and nothing in the inventory answers during the sleep, so a second sleep can no longer start inside the first.

- Loading a game no longer starts the poison's minute over, nor hurts a starving or exhausted character at once, and the poison line in the stats panel is now green.

- Easy is now closer to the original: blows and missiles that hit you do half damage instead of two thirds and you hit a little more often, while poison, traps, falls and spells hurt in full, so poison is no longer harmless on Easy; the monsters still take half again from every hit, to keep fights short. Passing out drunk in a fight now kills on Easy as on Standard, and at any point of the fight, not only while the plain combat music plays.

- The cudgel and the three maces are held lower while you charge a blow, so the head of the maces no longer runs off the top of the screen.

- The rock behind Achievements and the acknowledgements is no longer squashed, and stands in the same place on every page of the title screen.

- The music can play on an external MT-32: the Munt emulator with its ROMs, or a real one behind a MIDI interface. Choose MT-32 EXT MIDI as the soundfont, and the tracks are sent exactly what the original sent a Roland MT-32, so they sound as they did on one.

- On the pad, Select no longer opens the map over the sleep or repair question, where A on the map answered Yes unseen and could leave you unable to move. Left or right on the last skill of character creation no longer empties the screen, and an A pressed on the map or a rune no longer picks up an item in the inventory.

- Name Enchantment cast on an item in the inventory now shows the same rising runes and white mist as an item named in the world.

- On the pad, a wand used from the inventory with Y now waits, ready, and the attack trigger fires it at the crosshair while the right stick aims, a wand of missiles shot after shot until B puts it away; before, each shot was a held Y with the thumb off the stick. Name Enchantment and its wand can now name an item picked with A in the inventory, which on the pad worked only on things lying in the world.

- The arrow keys walk with the inventory or the magic panel open, as W, A, S and D do, instead of moving a selection there that could not be seen.

- On the pad, holding up in the inventory stops at the top row instead of running on into the body slots, and an item picked up with A to move it stays picked up when you go up to the body slots and back.

- On the map, the pad's quill moves at the same speed at any frame rate and on any screen, as fast as the stick is tilted; the d-pad's left and right jump from note to note. A note is picked anywhere on its text, with the mouse too; picked up, it drops onto the quill's tip instead of leaping to put its start under it, and a cancel puts it back where it was, without closing the map on Esc.

- The on-screen keyboard has an Enter key, so the pad and the mouse can confirm a name, a save or a note from the keys, and a full stop and a dash beside it; Delete clears the text. A click outside the keyboard no longer confirms what you typed, except on the map, where the note goes where you click.

- More screens now answer the keyboard: any key ends a conversation, the how-many box takes a typed number and Enter (the arrows step it, Home takes one, End the whole stack), character creation moves with the arrows and Enter, the arrows turn the credits pages, and Esc skips the logos straight to the title menu.

- With the game menu, the map, a conversation or a dialog open, the pad's buttons and the Use key no longer act on what is in front of you, and a click there no longer throws the item on the cursor. Esc on the repair dialog or the flute now closes only that, not the inventory too.

- Cutscenes are now shown whole at the original's proportions, with black bands where the screen is another shape: the map and the death's skull no longer lose their top on a widescreen, the intro no longer loses its bottom on an ultrawide, and the logos and the title are no longer stretched.

- Monitor, Display mode (borderless or exclusive fullscreen, or windowed) and Resolution rows in the game menu's DISPLAY section, each size with its aspect ratio; a new display is kept only if you confirm it within 10 seconds, and each mode remembers its own size. A first start now fills the main monitor, instead of opening a 1920x1080 window that could run off a smaller one, and Alt-Enter only switches fullscreen, no longer also choosing a menu entry or starting to type runes.

- A Field of view row in the game menu's DISPLAY section widens or narrows the view, from 75 to 120 degrees across, and the weapon in your hand keeps its size and place. A 16:10 or 4:3 screen now sees as wide as a widescreen, with more height above and below, where it used to see less across.

- A Camera bob row in the game menu's DISPLAY section sets how much the view rises and falls as you walk, from off to twice as much as before.

- A new Classic keys button on the game menu's Controls page sets the keyboard to the original's: W to run and S to walk forward, A and D to turn, Z and C to step aside, X to step back, E and Q to fly up and down, 1, 2 and 3 to look down, ahead and up, F7 for the inventory and the stats, J to jump and Shift-J for the standing long jump. In the usual layout T and G fly up and down, Shift+Space is the standing long jump and F7 opens the inventory, then the stats; any key can now be cleared with Delete, and a right click on the rune bag opens the magic panel.

- F9, or right on the D-pad, uses the Track skill at once, as in the original: it tells you of the creatures within eight tiles and in which direction, or that there are none. Track still reports by itself every few seconds as well.

- Every key and pad button of play can now be changed, on the game menu's Controls maps: pick an action, press Enter or A, then the new key or button, and the action that had it takes the old one, so nothing is ever left without a key. Keys can be combined with Ctrl, Shift or Alt, the mouse's middle and side buttons can be used, the pad has its own buttons for walking, the inventory, the magic panel and the map, and whatever is on the triggers still changes sides with a left-handed character. The tutorial and the panels' hints name the keys you chose.

- The pad's short rumble when you run at a ledge, a warning before the edge, is now explained on the pad's controls map, and a Ledge rumble row turns it off, leaving the other rumbles alone.

- With a PlayStation pad the game shows its buttons - the cross, circle, square and triangle, L1, R1, L2, R2, Options and Share - in every prompt and on the controls map, which also draws that pad with its d-pad above the left stick. A Button prompts row can also set Xbox or PlayStation by hand.

- The game menu's Controls tab now draws the keyboard and the gamepad with what every key and button does, the pad a page for each part of the game - walking, the inventory, the magic panel, the map and the menus - and the right stick's look speed can be set. F1, or holding Start on the pad, opens the menu straight on them.

- Three more of the remake's additions can be turned off in the game menu's GAMEPLAY section, for the original's way: the automatic Search, Traps and Track reports, sprinting with its stamina gauge, and the items hidden in piles of debris, which are then only scenery. The music now pauses five to ten seconds between exploring tracks, and the pause can be set from zero up to five minutes. If you had already saved your options in an earlier build, your setting stays as it was, with no pause: change it in the game menu's AUDIO section. Saves, autosaves included, can be deleted from the game menu's Save and Load tabs, and Journey Onward on the title screen opens the same list of saves.

- The save and options panel is now a full-screen game menu with four tabs, Save, Load, Options and Controls, its options grouped in sections and applied as soon as they change, with Reset, Reset all and Undo changes in place of Apply All. Settings that could only be changed in the registry now have a row - autosave, the music over the map, the silence between tracks, the scale of the interface above 1080p, and the remake's gameplay additions, which the Classic rules button turns back to the original's way. The title screen's Setup opens the same menu, and the music tracks are a page of it, now reachable in a game too.

- The interface now follows the size of the screen: it keeps its size from 1366x768 to 1920x1080, grows evenly on larger screens with the edges of its pixel art softened, and shrinks as a whole on smaller ones such as a Steam Deck, where panels used to overlap; below 1280x720 a message says so once. Conversations fit a 768-line screen, and the list of saves on the title screen is wider and cuts long names short.

- A door spiked shut, with an iron spike or the Strengthen Door spell, now shows its spikes on your side, gone when you open it: the first iron spike goes by the lock, every further one and the spell's five anywhere along the frame, up to ten a door. The more spikes, the more blows the door takes to break, and the blows knock them out one by one; a look or the cursor calls it a spiked door, it stays spiked after a save and load, a portcullis or a secret door can no longer be spiked, and iron spikes used with the mouse are now used up, one a door, the rest staying in hand.

- When the music changes, on opening the map for instance, the last notes of the old track now fade out with their own release and echo instead of stopping dead, as in the original.

- With the MT-32 sound font, the timpani can be heard again: they played far too low to hear in most tracks, Wanderer among them. The map's music no longer opens on two seconds of near silence: its soft opening pad plays as loud as on an MT-32.

## Release UUBuild15Nov2026.zip

- The Steam copy of the original game is accepted: its `game.bin` is unpacked like the GoG copy's `game.gog`. On Windows a GoG or Steam copy is found by itself the first time the game starts, wherever it was installed; Auto Search looks further, in every Steam library also under Proton, and in `C:\UW` and `C:\DOSBox` for the DOS game.

- The game folder dialog shows Validating... or Searching... while it works, no longer freezes while it unpacks game.gog, and unpacks it again if that was cut short.

- Your own mp3 and ogg music tracks no longer freeze the game for a moment when one starts: a long track used to stop it for about a second.

- Opening the prisoner's cell on level 3 yourself, with a lockpick or the Open spell, now frees him as in the original: talk to him and he dances his thanks and leaves, and the lizardman by his cell turns hostile. The lizardman language achievement now comes only from freeing him through the lizardman.

- Conversations that make another creature friendly or hostile now do so, as in the original: making peace with a bandit on level 3 also calms his companion.

- Taking what belongs to a group angers those of its members who can see you do it, up to seven or nine squares away for goblins, instead of any within three squares, even through a wall. The goblins by two locked doors on level 1 react when the door is opened, not when its lock is picked.

- Unlocking a door no longer sets off something unrelated elsewhere on the level, such as a secret door on a later level opening by itself.

- Browse in the game folder dialog now opens the system's Open dialog, also on Linux and the Steam Deck: pick game.gog or UW.EXE in the game's folder.

- The Made with Unity screen at start-up can be cut short with any key, a click or a pad button.

- The title screens and character creation no longer run the graphics card flat out: they keep to the screen's refresh rate, as the game does.

## Release UUBuild2Nov2026.zip

- The three buttons by the stairs on level 3 work as in the original: the right order opens the door beside them, and a wrong press brings a skeleton at the far end of the room, as many times as it is made.

- The map fills in with what your light shows as you go, as in the original: in the dark only the spot you stand on, with a torch a narrow trail, a wider one with Night Vision or Daylight, and nothing at all in the Void. Each new tile on the map pays experience that grows with the depth: the first level explored whole pays about a sixth of what it did, from the fourth down one and a half to almost three times as much, a little more than before over the whole dungeon, and the Void nothing.

- A secret door not yet found no longer shows on the map as a gap in the wall.

- Loading a save no longer leaves the map pages of other levels drawn as they were in the game played before.

- The magic panel now opens with a parchment on the right listing the spells you know, by circle, each with its runes: at first the forty in the manual, then each of the others as you read its runes in a book or hear them in a conversation, or first cast it. A message tells you when one is added.

- The Flameproof spell is now called Resist Fire, as in the manual, wherever the game shows its name, on enchanted items and potions too.

- Creatures swing as often as in the original, about every two and a quarter seconds instead of every three and a third, so fights are shorter and harder: the first goblin of the dungeon swings half again as often. A creature's critical blow now makes you flinch, with a harder shake, rumble and grunt, and sets back the attack you are charging, to the start of the red if it was charged, as your own critical blows already cost a creature its swing; and a creature always cries out at your critical blows.

- Experience is earned as in the original: once your level is more than twice the depth plus two (above 4 on the first level), every gain is halved, and a marked creature pays about twice as much. Only your own kills pay, the silver sapling costs an eighth of your experience instead of a fifth, and a creature the dungeon brings back pays less each time it returns from the same place, and carries no loot after the first.

- The Standard of Honor now fits in a pack, box or chest, and can be carried on a shoulder or in the shield hand, as in the original.

- A torch, candle, taper or lantern can be carried in the shield hand as well as on a shoulder, and burns down there as it does on a shoulder. Lighting one that sits in your pack puts it on a free shoulder or in the free hand; a light no longer burns for a minute in the pack.

- With a bag or chest open, a right click on its icon above the items puts what you are holding back outside it, and the bag stays open; a left click still closes it. Closing a bag brings back the list it was opened from as you left it, instead of scrolling the bag to the last row, and a bag opens where you left it. Arrows, coins or food put into a closed bag join the same pile inside instead of making a second one.

- Escape on the messagebox that asks how many to take or move now closes only that messagebox, and the inventory stays open, instead of opening the save panel or closing the inventory.

- The mouse wheel scrolls the inventory, an open bag or chest, and the text of a conversation the right way round: turning it down goes down.

- Burning incense no longer plays a dungeon track under the vision: the music falls silent while the vision lasts and starts again when it ends.

- Setup, a new entry on the title menu, lets you choose the music for each moment of the game, from exploring and combat to the map and the title screen: the original tracks, your own MP3, OGG or WAV files, or both, one picked at random when there are several; each can be listened to there, through any of the soundfonts, and switched off without removing it. The choice is kept in a music.txt you can also edit by hand, and the sequel's ambient tracks, if you had added them, are already in it.

- Changing the soundfont, in the options or on the Setup screen, no longer starts the music over: the track carries on from where it was, with the new instruments, and the title music keeps looping.

- In the Introduction a left click or A goes on to the next scene and Escape or Start goes back to the menu; in the Acknowledgements a left click anywhere turns to the next page, a right click to the previous one, and Escape goes back. Both screens now show their keys. Escape also ends a dream and the two story scenes of level 7.

- A mage no longer casts a fireball or a lightning bolt through the creature standing in front of it, which killed its companion in the blast.

- An acid slug's spit now flies in an arc and falls, as in the original, carrying four tiles at most. A mage's thrown knife flies in an arc too, faster and flatter.

- An imp no longer freezes in mid-air after a heavy blow, hovering in front of you and doing nothing for the rest of the fight. A goblin, rat or other creature standing about fidgets now and then again, not just once.

- A creature you crowd in a fight backs away twice as fast as before, as in the original, and keeps backing while it swings at you. A sling goblin that gets clear takes up its sling and shoots at once. A goblin, imp, gazer, fire elemental or acid slug whose missile misses walks closer, still shooting, until one reaches you; mages keep their distance. Only two of the three goblin types that slung still do: the third carries a club.

- A spell waiting for its target can be put away with a right click or Esc. The right click used to swing the weapon as well.

- Hold left Shift while the inventory or magic panel is open, or while a spell waits for its target, and the mouse turns the view again, so you can line up a throw or a spell. A click then aims at the crosshair in the middle of the screen; let go of Shift and the pointer is back.

- When you ready a spell, its red circle now sits exactly where the pointer's cross was, instead of a little up and to the right.

- The Main Menu button on the save and load screen is now solid wood inside, instead of showing the dungeon through it.

- Loading a save no longer moves a lever, switch or pull chain onto a door on another level: the pull chain at the start of the dungeon could leave its wall after a save made deeper down. Saves where it already moved put it back when loaded.

- Near an anvil, items it can never repair - bows, crossbows, slings, rings, crowns and the like - are thrown as usual, and the pad no longer offers to repair them.

- The dungeon brings creatures back with time, as in the original: about every half hour a spot at least eight tiles away may get its creature back, and a long time away from a level, or a night's sleep, usually fills them all. Each spot keeps at most one such creature alive, so a level never fills up.

- Creating a character rolls as the original does: the first point on a skill is worth about twice the second - a Fighter who takes Attack twice ends near 13 instead of 18 - no skill starts above 30, and the attributes spread wider, one sometimes staying at the class minimum. A new character also starts with full hit points.

- The game no longer freezes when you answer "Why not?" to the lizardman Sseetharee on level 3. The conversation ends there, as in the original.

- F uses what you point at, like Y on the pad: a door or a lever works at once, and an item goes straight into your pack instead of onto the cursor, with the inventory opening on it. With an item already on the cursor, F puts it in your pack.

- The number beside the paperdoll now says what it is: point at it, or move onto it with the d-pad, and the top of the panel reads "carry weight left". On the d-pad the two hands now name their numbers too, as they did under the pointer.

- A white line under the worn spells shows the stamina left for sprinting. It gets longer as Acrobat grows, shortens while you sprint and fills back up when you stop. The worn spell icons are a little smaller to make room for it. This line and those of the timed spells have a dark track behind them for the part used up.

- The Rune of Warding hurts the enemy that sets it off, more with a higher Casting skill, as in the original, though a creature it kills gives no experience. Only hostile creatures set it off, the warning says where the creature is, you can lay more than one, and with a wall too close in front of you there is no room for it.

- A creature that a trap makes again every time you set it off, like the skeleton behind the three buttons on level 3, gives experience and loot only the first time. The creatures the dungeon brings back by itself are worth fighting as before.

- When the title cutscene ends, the menu starts on Journey Onward with the pointer on it, or on Create Character when there are no saves yet. The click or A that skips the two logos also skips the title cutscene.

- Six potions and a scroll that worked as Leap are Hallucination, as in the original: "Your vision distorts and you feel light headed.", and the world swims for about a minute, like a mushroom's but at once; a save now keeps either running. The enchanted loaf of bread no longer cures poison, which in the original it never did.

- A spell that backfires costs its mana, as in the original, and hurts more the higher its circle, up to 4d8 on the eighth: enough to kill a weak caster.

- Sleeping heals as in the original, and restores your mana too: well fed on a bedroll you wake with about two thirds of both back, otherwise about a third, and starving with nothing. A creature hunting nearby may come and wake you after a few hours ("Your sleep is interrupted!"), and on waking a line tells you how long you slept and how rested and hungry you are.

- F10, or holding left on the D-pad, lies down to sleep anywhere, as in the original: a bedroll anywhere in your pack makes it a better night. The game asks before you sleep, and a hostile creature only keeps you awake when it is hunting you within two tiles.

- Passing out from drink is a real night's sleep, with its healing and its risks, and passing out in water hurts, as in the original. Passing out with a hostile creature nearby is still death.

- While you sleep, three open doors in ten swing shut, near ones as well, and a mushroom's visions end, as in the original.

- Mages cast from afar and no longer die of their own fireballs: they keep their distance, back away slowly when you close in, and up close throw a knife or strike with the staff instead. Creatures that back away from you now walk backwards. As in the original, a spell, an arrow or a sling stone with no room to leave its caster is not released: you get "There is not enough room to release that spell." and keep your mana, or "You need more space to fire that weapon."

- Creatures run from a fight as in the original, by their courage and their wounds: mages and goblins break and flee, skeletons and golems fight on unless one blow takes half their life, and one you shoot from afar charges you instead. A creature that has fled keeps away from you, fights back when cornered, and calms down once it can no longer see or hear you.

- A creature that a light blow only upset no longer starts a fight after flinching from it.

- Striking a creature alerts the others of its kind around it, as in the original: they react at once, some creatures from as far as 9 tiles away.
